Project Description
Design Summary
A detailed project description can be found in the Project Plan, PLN-P01-060. The MX1 Portable X-Ray System (“MX1”) was originally intended to be cleared under 510(k) K231372; however, MedAI received an NSE letter from the FDA declaring that the design was not considered to be substantially equivalent to marketed products. MedAI is pursuing a new submission with design updates to address the Agency’s concerns. The original design differences as compared to MedAI’s first generation device, Imager P00, are described in PLN-P01-022. The primary differences between the original MX1 design and the current design include a new radioscopy mode (fluoro mode) and additional safety features in the device software.  Refer to Project Plan PLN-P01-060 and individual product BOM for additional detail.
Battery Powered Operation
The emitter can be charged via the wireless charger or wired charger. The Wired Charger draws power from AC mains. The emitter can emit x-rays while docked to the Wireless Charger but can NOT emit x-rays while wired charging; it can operate otherwise. The Wireless Charger momentarily halts charging during x-ray emission. The cassette is charged via the wired charger. The cassette can capture x-rays while charging.

Planned Intended Use, Indications, and Contraindications
The following Intended Use, Indications for Use, and Contraindications are currently planned for initial regulatory submissions; they may be altered during the course of development dependent on final product design, testing, or feedback from regulatory authorities.
Intended Use: The MX1 Portable X-ray System is designed to aid clinicians with point-of-care visualization and guidance during X-rays of extremities and shoulders. It is intended for use in clinical environments and is not intended for surgical applications.
Indications for Use: The MX1 Portable X-ray System is indicated for use by qualified/trained medical professionals on adult patients for orthopedic radiographic, orthopedic serial radiographic, orthopedic fluoroscopic, and orthopedic interventional procedures of only shoulders to fingers and knees to toes. The device is not intended for use during surgery. The device is not intended to replace a stationary radiographic system.
The device is to be used in healthcare facilities where qualified operators are present (e.g., outpatient clinics, urgent cares, imaging centers, sports medicine facilities, occupational medicine clinics)The device is not intended to be used in environments with the following characteristics:
Aseptic or sterile fields, such as in surgery
Home or residential settings or other settings where qualified operators are not present
Vehicular and moving environments
Environments under direct sunlight
Oxygen-rich environments, such as near an operating oxygenation concentrator
Contraindications
The MX1 System is NOT intended for bariatric patients.
The MX1 System is NOT intended for mammography.
The MX1 System is NOT intended for dental applications.
The MX1 System is NOT intended to come in contact with non-intact skin.
The MX1 System is NOT intended for cardiac applications.
The MX1 System is NOT intended for use in proximity to pacemakers or implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs).

SAR Testing
Per 47 CFR 1.1310, Specific absorption rate (SAR) shall be used to evaluate the environmental impact of human exposure to radiofrequency (RF) radiation as specified in § 1.1307(b) within the frequency range of 100 kHz to 6 GHz (inclusive). The SAR limits for general population/uncontrolled exposure are 0.08 W/kg, as averaged over the whole body, and a peak spatial-average SAR of 1.6 W/kg, averaged over any 1 gram of tissue (defined as a tissue volume in the shape of a cube). Exceptions are the parts of the human body treated as extremities, such as hands, wrists, feet, ankles, and pinnae, where the peak spatial-average SAR limit is 4 W/kg, averaged over any 10 grams of tissue (defined as a tissue volume in the shape of a cube). Exposure may be averaged over a time period not to exceed 30 minutes to determine compliance with general population/uncontrolled SAR limits.At operating frequencies above 6 GHz, the MPE limits listed in Table 3 shall be used to evaluate the environmental impact of human exposure to RF radiation; limits listed are for continuous exposure, that is, for indefinite time periods. Backscatter Shield
Some city and state regulations require the use of an “external backscatter shield” on handheld x-ray equipment.  The use of an optional physical shield supplied with the equipment is described in the FDA guidance Radiation Safety Considerations for X-ray Equipment Designed for Hand-held Use (2008), although the FDA does not require it.  Such an external backscatter shield would be used in conjunction with operator shielding, lead, and/or lead-equivalent PPE.  Per the guidance document and the regulations of various localities, MedAI believes the intent of these regulations is to reduce operator exposure to backscatter and, most importantly, be within the bounds of the occupational dose limits.  The occupational dose limit in the United States set by 10 CFR 20 Subpart C is 5.0 rem/year, or 0.5 rem/year for a declared pregnant woman.
MedAI does not intend to include an “external backscatter shield”, and rather intends to develop a device with sufficiently low backscatter radiation that allows for use within Occupational Dose Limits. Scatter radiation data, as well operator safety information, shall be sufficiently described in the instruction for use.
The reason for excluding the use of an external backscatter shield is that it could negatively impact usability by increasing the size and weight of the device. Notably, some x-ray devices provide removable external backscatter shields to meet the state requirement. The use of removable external backscatter shields increases the risk of misuse if the shield is not attached, and may inadvertently lead to exceeding Occupational Dose Limits if the device relies on use of the backscatter shield for operator safety.
