Protocol Section
STUDY PURPOSE
This document describes the Usability Summative Evaluation of the MX1 Portable X-ray System (“MX1”) as a part of the design validation activities to determine whether the MX1 has acceptable use-related risk and effectiveness.
OBJECTIVE
To identify if the operator can interpret the Instructions for Use (IFU) and operate the device with minimum use-related hazards, as well as to observe the operator interactions with the MX1 Portable X-ray System. The summative evaluation shall demonstrate and provide evidence that the MX1 System can be used safely and effectively.

METHODS
Locations and Personnel Responsibilities
MedAI Engineering and Quality personnel are responsible for execution of the protocol.
Overview
Participants shall be verified as qualified operators. MedAI shall retain a copy of each participant’s license number or training record, as applicable.
The test participant shall use the MX1 system in a realistic use scenario, but may not include all simulated conditions of use.
Each participant will be provided with an adequately charged MX1 Portable X-ray System and accompanying Instructions for Use.
Participants will be asked to read the appropriate sections of the IFU as they perform certain tasks using the MX1 system.
While performing, participants are free to consult the IFU as many times as necessary to complete the task. MedAI personnel may answer the test participant’s questions, as needed, but these questions shall be recorded and evaluated to determine any necessary IFU updates.
All test participants shall wear a protective lead apron when using Radiograph mode on the device.
MedAI personnel shall observe and record the actions required to complete the task and if there are any deviations from the instructions provided in the IFU.
Analyzing Results
The following parameters shall be considered for the validation process:
Difficulties and use-errors the operator experienced during the tasks and their root cause.
For the sterile bagging steps, whether the sterile field was maintained throughout bagging.
The risk and potential clinical consequences of use-errors.
If any additional use-error mitigations need to be considered (e.g. IFU revision).
If the design, the labeling, or the IFU needs to be revised.
X-RAY OPERATION TEST STEPS AND OBSERVATIONS
Rationale for Observations (Tasks) Selected for Summative Evaluation
All risks from RSK-P01-003 Sections 6 (use errors) and 7 (user interface risks), as well as any user interface related risks from other sections as applicable, were considered for inclusion in this summative evaluation protocol. All risks that could be reasonably tested through usability and task performance were included in the protocol, regardless of risk severity level. Risks that could not be evaluated through direct observation of task performance and/or that were identified in IFU Warnings and Cautions were addressed through knowledge questions asked during the post-task interview.
In addition to risks from the MX1 risk analysis as described in Section 7.1.1 above, MX1 Primary Operating Functions as listed in MEMO-P01-426 Rev. B were also included in this protocol. The definition of Primary Operating Functions per IEC 62366-1 is tasks the user must accomplish to fulfill functions related to the Essential Performance and safety. Observations marked with “ *** ” are associated with primary operating functions as defined in the MEMO-P01-426 Rev. B User Interface Specification. All primary operating functions were tested through usability and/or task performance.
Sections 7.5.1 through 7.5.9 describe the test steps and verbal instructions that will be given by MedAI test personnel. Corresponding observations shall be recorded in the tables.
All x-ray operators shall wear a lead apron while utilizing the MX1 in radiographic mode.
All push tests within this protocol are completed with the assumption that 50N over 5 seconds (using a Force Gauge and Stopwatch) appropriately encompasses any force (e.g. bump) that could result in the operator dropping the Emitter or Cassette during normal use.

The Emitter screen displays a capture assistance tool we call “Viewfinder”. Viewfinder can help you see your image parameters before acquiring, as well as change them. You are seeing a live video stream of the Emitter’s camera, and the three buttons below can be used to change loading factors.

There are other parameters like SID and SSD displayed on the tablet, when actively capturing. SID is the x-ray Source-to-Image Distance which is related to the distance between the Emitter and Cassette, and SSD is the Source-to-Skin Distance or distance between the Emitter and anatomy. The MX1 system uses infrared lights and LIDAR technology to know the Emitter’s position relative to the Cassette. Knowing the position lets the system safely prevent x-ray emissions when dangerously off target. For the ideal position, the Emitter should be:
between 25 and 80 cm from the Cassette (SID),
no closer than 20 cm from the anatomy (SSD), and
Pointing towards the center of the Active Area.
When you do this, the automatic collimator will shrink or grow in size to fill the active area and the system will indicate it is ready to fire with green lights. Otherwise, they will be red.
Please position the Emitter above the Cassette again. Move the Emitter around so that the LED lights change from green to red and get a sense of the Tracking System interlocks. Then demonstrate a position that will allow x-ray emission.

PROTOCOL DEVIATIONS
Cassette Backend Configuration Loaded
The cassette supplied by the Design Team was not properly configured to match the software version release of the Device App on the tablet. This was not known until the MedAI test administration personnel had traveled to participants. The result was an inability to enter patient information as part of beginning an image exam, thus an inability to complete step 7.3.4.2 in the protocol. To execute the study within the time scheduled for the study, the first 6 participants did not undergo step 7.3.4.2, and the remainder of the protocol was followed as written. After the first 6 participants, the correct software configuration file was replaced onto the Cassette and the system was rebooted. The cassette successfully opened to the patient information enter page, and participants thereafter completed step 7.3.4.2. This step was completed successfully for all 24 participants that attempted it; this sample size was sufficient to demonstrate the step could be completed successfully.
Foot Pedal Not Paired
The foot pedal supplied by the Design Team was not properly paired to the system. This was not known until the MedAI test administration personnel had traveled to participants. To execute the study within the time scheduled for the study, the following test steps replaced steps 7.3.5.7 - 7.3.5.9:
The MX1 system comes with an optional foot pedal for triggering x-rays, among other functions. We have the Foot Pedal here in a protective bag, but not paired to the system. Based on the labeling on the foot pedal, and Foot Pedal information in Section 5 of the IFU, can you identify which of these pedals you believe should trigger x-rays like the trigger on the Emitter? [User passes if they identify the right pedal]
Can you demonstrate with your foot how you would capture a single radiography? [User passes if they tap the right pedal]
Can you demonstrate with your foot how you would capture a dynamic digital radiography (DDR)? [User passes if they tap and hold the right pedal]
There were no incorrect answers or improper uses for these steps. These passing user demonstrations related to the foot pedal satisfy the mitigations for risks documented in 7.3.5.7 - 7.3.5.9
Forward Orientation Supplied Picture
While testing the first few users, it became apparent that a picture was required to educate the user in how to hold the Emitter in the forward orientation to complete steps within 7.3.5. The MedAI test personnel observed that 12 users among the first 15 were visibly confused about how to hold the Emitter in the forward orientation or attempted improper holding techniques. In these cases, a brief demonstration was shown to the user, and the protocol proceeded normally.
To evaluate the impact of including a picture in the IFU demonstrating how to hold the Emitter in the forward orientation, the below picture was shown to the user (for users 16-30) on a smartphone during the directions in 7.3.5.1. The protocol then proceeded normally without demonstration.
After beginning to show the image to the later participants (n=15), no further confusion or incorrect attempts to hold the Emitter in the forward orientation were observed. This image, or similar, will be added to the MX1 Instructions for Use.

When asked “Were you able to recognize when the device was emitting radiation?”, two participants responded no. Further clarification was requested and it was determined their responses were due to uncertainty in the timing of the radiation emission during and after trigger press. Earlier in the procedure, both participants confirmed they were not confused by the LED lights or could hear the beeps during an x-ray emission. Further information is provided below:
Mr. Wood clarified his uncertainty was due to the delay after the trigger press for the brief yellow lights & buzzer to activate. The MX1 System has a delay after trigger press of approximately 400 ms, and the yellow lights and buzzer activate very briefly for single exposure due to the device's short exposure times (40-200 ms). Mr. Wood clarified he understood an x-ray was being taken, and just was not sure of the exact timing when the x-ray was being emitted between trigger press and the yellow lights and buzzer activation.
Mr. Powers clarified while pressing the trigger he preferred to focus on the Tablet for the images to appear, rather than pay attention to the x-ray emission indicators (lights and beeps), and thus was not sure of the exact timing of the x-ray emission. Mr. Powers confirmed earlier in the study that he did hear the beeps and was not confused by the LED lights.

CONCLUSION
This study was conducted in order to evaluate the overall usability of the MX1 system related to risks and user experience. All users were able to properly unpack, power on, and use the system with relative ease. All Moderate level risks associated with user errors in this study have been addressed with new mitigations. New mitigations were also added for select Broadly Acceptable level risks, as documented in Table 6 of this report. The MX1 Risk Analysis (RSK-MX1-003) will be updated to reflect the observed use error frequencies as documented in Table 4 of this report, and the new mitigations listed in Table 6 will be added. All participants were generally happy with the device’s portability, capability, and accessibility. Finally, this study supplied user feedback to consider for future MedAI projects and products.
