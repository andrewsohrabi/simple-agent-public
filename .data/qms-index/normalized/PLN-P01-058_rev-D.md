Purpose
This document provides the Design Transfer Plan for the MX1 Portable X-Ray System (“MX1”) and its accessories, the F1 Foot Pedal, and the K1 Cart. The Cart contains the Wireless Charger Assembly (W1).
Scope

The MX1 Portable X-Ray System (DHF-008).
The F1 Foot Pedal (DHF-008).
The K1 Cart (DHF-003).
The W1 Wireless Charger Assembly, MS-10514 (DHF-006).

Roles and Responsibilities
Quality Engineering
Quality Engineering is responsible for maintaining and enforcing the Design Control Procedure, QSP-018, as well as ensuring that all design transfer activities comply with ISO 13485:2016 and 21 CFR 820.30(h). Quality Engineering will also review required document deliverables and facilitate the transfer of released production specification documents to all manufacturing sites in scope of transfer..
Engineering
Engineering is responsible for generating documentation pertaining to product design, manufacturing fixture design, and manufacturing work instructions.
Operations
Operations serves as the main point of contact between MedAI and the manufacturing sites. They are responsible for coordinating the Design Transfer process and managing the transfer of materials to the manufacturing sites. Additionally, Operations will collaborate with Quality Engineering to facilitate the transfer of production specification documents to the manufacturing sites.
Manufacturing Site
The Manufacturing Site is responsible for manufacturing the products contractually agreed upon by MedAI and the Manufacturing Site. The Manufacturing Site will implement all Manufacturing Site-related documentation in accordance with the site-specific Quality Plan, as applicable.
Finished Device Manufacturing Sites
Sanmina Corporation
Sanmina is the Contract Manufacturing Organization (CMO) selected by MedAI to manufacture the MX1 Portable X-ray System. Sanmina is located in Carrollton, Texas and is an ISO 13485-certified and FDA-registered facility. FDA Registration # 3009609218.
Sanmina will create the pFMEA, execute process validation, and maintain manufacturing documentation as agreed upon in the Quality Plan, Sanmina document 555-0005-Q.
MX1 will be produced on a production line that includes hand assembly stations and Workstations. There will be approximately 15 Workstations that will perform in-process testing and assess subassembly performance during the manufacturing of the MX1 system.
Steute Meditech
MedAI selected Steute as the CMO of the MX1 Foot Pedal (F1). Stuete is located in Loehne, Germany and is an ISO 13485-certified and FDA-registered facility. FDA Registration # 3015515531. Although requested, Steute was unwilling to be the legal manufacturer of F1 due to the custom PCBA design because Steute typically provides customers with their own design. Therefore, all F1s must be received by MedAI before release for distribution.
Steute manufactures the foot pedal housing, and will produce the F1 assembly using at least 1 Workstation. MedAI will provide all initial assembly manufacturing work instructions and MedAI Workstation components. Steute maintains its own equipment for testing the IP (Ingress Protection) rating of units after assembly.
MedAI will be manufacturing up to the first 90 foot pedal assemblies while Stuete establishes the F1 manufacturing line. Following manufacturing line setup, Steute will produce the F1 Foot Pedal, but MedAI will still be listed as the legal manufacturer under MedAI’s FDA Registration # 3018077344.
GCX Healthcare Solutions
MedAI selected GCX as the CMO of the K1 Cart. GCX is located in El Paso, Texas and is an ISO 13485-certified and FDA-registered facility. FDA Registration # 3006128321.
The general responsibilities of both MedAI and GCX  are defined in PLN-P01-046 - GCX OEM Supplier Quality Plan.
GCX will develop and maintain a manufacturing process in accordance with the Cart design specifications. MedAI will not be developing Workstations or providing manufacturing materials to GCX.
MedAI, Inc
MedAI will manufacture the Wireless Charger Assembly and consign the finished assembly to GCX for assembly on the K1 Cart.
MedAI shall create and maintain the pFMEA, execute process validation, and ensure Good Manufacturing Practices are followed during manufacturing.
The Wireless Charger Assembly will be manufactured at the MedAI facility (100 Main St, Ste 700, Springfield, IL 60001) on an assembly line that includes hand assembly stations and 1 Workstation for assembly verification.
Strategy
Design transfer occurs during Phase 4 of development per QSP-018 - Design Controls. MedAI may begin Phase 4 activities in parallel to Phase 3 activities after all design Verification and Validation has been completed, and Verification and Validation reports have been released.
To formally initiate design transfer, Quality Engineering may host a Design Transfer Kickoff Meeting with all internal stakeholders. The stakeholders shall agree upon the strategy and deliverables outlined in this document in order to commence design transfer.
During design transfer, the MX1 product design must be properly translated into production specifications and capabilities that meet product requirements. To ensure that production specifications have been well defined, the following deliverables must be completed:
Design Outputs (drawings, specifications, etc)
Risk Management File, including the pFMEA
Inspection Procedures
Process Specifications
Equipment and Tool Specifications
Manufacturing Training Requirements
Manufacturing Process Flowchart
Manufacturing Work Instructions
Process Validation
Documentation and Records Requirements
Post Market Surveillance Plan
Design outputs shall be reviewed during Design Control Phase Reviews. Product specifications, drawings, and requirements are maintained in the Device History File.
Document deliverables will be uploaded by Quality Engineering to a quality-controlled folder in the MedAI Google Drive that the Manufacturing Site can access for products where manufacturing is outsourced.
Transfer Review and Approval
The design transfer shall be reviewed upon Design Control Phase 4 closure. The manufacturing process shall be demonstrated to reliably produce products that meet specifications.
MedAI personnel shall review all Phase 4 deliverables via Design Phase review per QSP-018 - Design Control.
Change Management
All modifications made during the transfer process shall be documented and approved per QSP-002 - Document Control.
Any changes made to the product design during design transfer shall follow the change management steps outlined in Section 5 of the Design Control Procedure, QSP-018.
Where changes are initiated by the Manufacturing Site, the Manufacturing Site shall be responsible for change control within their QMS and maintaining records according to their procedures. Changes requiring MedAI approval are detailed in the site-specific Quality Plans.
For all MedAI-maintained designs, MedAI shall initiate changes that affect design, although the Manufacturing Site may request the change.
Documentation and Records
All documentation and records shall be maintained per the requirements described in QSP-016 - Quality Records.
Post-Market Surveillance
Post-market surveillance, continuous improvement, and monitoring are part of Design Control Phase 5, per QSP-018 - Design Control.
Phase 5 constitutes the formal exit from the design control process and triggers the beginning of the post production product life cycle. This phase requires continuous re-evaluation of risk based on the addition of new information from various sources such as production activities and customer feedback. Postmarket Surveillance activities are managed per WI-004, Post-Market Surveillance.
